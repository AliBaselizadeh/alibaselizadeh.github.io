Essays & notes

PHILOSOPHY OF COGNITIVE SCIENCE

Distal sensitivity and the mark of the noncognitive

A research essay asking whether sensitivity to absent conditions can distinguish cognition from sophisticated regulation in designed mechanisms. The argument compares causal, informational, and normative readings through Kalman filtering, internal-model control, and bacterial memory.

PHILOSOPHY OF COGNITIVE NEUROSCIENCE

From contribution to necessity: Motor signals, intentional binding, and the sense of agency

An analysis of the evidence for premotor contributions to agency and of the ambiguity between constitutive and production readings of necessity. It argues that intentional binding alone cannot settle the dispute with inferential accounts.

PREDICTIVE PROCESSING / ETHNOGRAPHY

Whose Inference? A critical review of predictive-processing accounts of spirit possession

A review of nine predictive-processing and active-inference accounts, arguing that they model the possessed individual’s inference while overlooking the public inference performed by ritual specialists.

TRANSLATION

Computational Psychiatry: A Primer — Persian translation

A nearly complete Persian translation of Peggy Seriès’ Computational Psychiatry: A Primer (MIT Press), with publication rights under discussion through a Persian publisher.
