02 — Research

Questions made testable

My work moves between conceptual analysis and formal modelling. I use concrete systems to test claims about cognition, explanation, and the boundary between the cognitive and the noncognitive.

01

Distal sensitivity and the mark of the noncognitive

A philosophy of cognitive science project separating causal, informational, and normative readings of distal sensitivity. The argument tests a proposed mark of the noncognitive against Kalman filters, internal-model controllers, and bacterial memory in E. coli.

02

Is the human explorer a Kalman filter?

A Bayesian reanalysis of open exploration data comparing Kalman-filter and delta-rule learners with hierarchical Bayesian probit models, PSIS-LOO, model recovery, and parameter recovery.

Public repository →
03

From contribution to necessity

A philosophy of cognitive neuroscience essay on premotor signals, intentional binding, and the sense of agency. It evaluates whether the evidence supports a contribution claim, a necessity claim, or an inferential account.

04

Whose inference?

A critical review of predictive-processing accounts of spirit possession, using Zar rituals in Hormozgan as a test case. A minimal multi-agent simulation examines how explicit claim-checking between ritual specialists changes identification accuracy.

with Assist. Prof. Maryam Gharasou, University of Tehran
05

Controllability, precision and block structure in pain ratings

A reanalysis of Habermann and Büchel’s pain-controllability study using the authors’ code and data, examining precision specifications, block-level validation, and the stability of the reported controllability effect.

with Amir Hakimjavadi, Universitat Rovira i Virgili
