About

I am an interdisciplinary researcher trained in electrical engineering and philosophy of science, currently studying Human-Centered AI at the University of Milan. My work spans cognitive neuroscience, computational psychiatry, consciousness science, philosophy of mind, and philosophy of cognitive science.

I study why the same measure or intervention behaves differently from one person to the next: how neural and bodily signals relate to cognition and mental health, how brain state shapes the response to brain stimulation, and what a measurement must show before it supports a claim about experience.

My methods combine reproducible EEG and fMRI analysis, Bayesian computational modelling, evidence synthesis, and philosophical analysis. Alongside clinical and consciousness-oriented projects, I am developing Distal sensitivity and the mark of the noncognitive, a philosophy project using control theory, state estimation, information theory, and concrete biological and engineered systems.
