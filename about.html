About

I work in philosophy of cognitive science and philosophy of neuroscience, with a master’s degree in philosophy of science and a bachelor’s degree in electrical engineering. My central question is what separates minimal cognition from the prediction and regulation found in designed mechanisms.

I ask whether sensitivity to absent conditions can mark the cognitive and, if it cannot, which facts about how a system produces and maintains its own states do that work. I approach this question through control theory, state estimation, information theory, and computational models fitted to human behaviour.

My proposed doctoral project is Distal sensitivity and the mark of the noncognitive. The project tests philosophical claims against engineered estimators, internal-model controllers, bacterial memory in E. coli, and models of human learning.
